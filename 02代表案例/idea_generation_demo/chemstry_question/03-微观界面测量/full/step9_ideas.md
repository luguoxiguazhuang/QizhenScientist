# Null-Flux Reciprocal Perturbation Microscopy for Measuring Nonlocal Interfacial Transport Kernels

## Motivation

Microscopic interface phenomena are currently measured as disconnected observables: structural layering by simultaneous neutron reflectivity, infrared spectroscopy, and ellipsometry [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308711154); local electrical and chemical-frictional heterogeneity by in situ conductive atomic force microscopy [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4321480595); functional wettability and mass transfer in gas-liquid-solid catalysis [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4223928347); and directional liquid-solid flow patterns [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3028689404). The unresolved bottleneck is not lack of individual observables but lack of a quantitative inverse measurement that converts them into absolute local flux-force coefficients, especially frequency-dependent and spatially nonlocal kernels, and then scales those local kernels to macroscopic evaporation, cooling, combustion, spray drying, or catalytic rates. The idea imports three cross-domain mechanisms: physics-based inverse modeling from computer-science sensor-data integration [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2921417107), accelerated uncertainty updating from stochastic transport in heterogeneous materials [Author et al., 2012](https://openalex.org/W1982185356), and conservative scalar-flux footprint integration from earth-science surface flux measurement [Author et al., 1999](https://openalex.org/W2167962621). It addresses the prior novelty gap by replacing generic Onsager regularization with an interface-specific reciprocal null-flux assay: selected perturbation combinations force a chosen cross-flux to zero, so the ratio of coupled coefficients is obtained from the cancellation condition, and frequency-coded perturbations recover dynamic memory and nonlocality that passive imaging cannot access.

## Method

This method is intended to solve the inability of current microscopic interface measurements to recover coupled local fluxes and scale them to operating device rates; it can be divided into 5 parts: correlative operando sensing, reciprocal null-flux perturbation, interface-specific nonlocal Onsager identifiability, physics-constrained inverse assimilation, and conservative flux upscaling. The primary testable hypothesis is that a structured reciprocal null-flux perturbation protocol recovers interfacial cross-coefficients and nonlocal response kernels with lower uncertainty and better held-out flux prediction than passive, single-perturbation, or randomized multi-perturbation protocols when the sample, sensors, and inversion model are held fixed. The first phase tests this hypothesis by varying only the perturbation protocol and keeping interface chemistry, measurement modalities, perturbation energy, and analysis pipeline constant.

**1. Correlative operando interfacial cell**

Build a liquid-solid cell with a planar silica, gold, or catalytic film, compatible with directional flow mapping [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3028689404), in situ conductive atomic force microscopy [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4321480595), and simultaneous reflectivity, infrared, and ellipsometry structural profiling [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308711154). For gas-liquid-solid extensions, add a controlled gas channel and characterize wettability and mass transfer as in triple-phase catalysis [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4223928347). The cell records local velocity, film thickness, density, roughness, local conductivity, electron transfer, and chemical-frictional contrast under the same operating conditions.

**2. Reciprocal null-flux perturbation engine**

Apply small-amplitude electric, thermal, concentration, and pressure perturbations through transparent electrodes, microheaters, concentration gates, or pressure controllers. Perturbations are encoded as reciprocal pairs or tuples chosen so that a target cross-flux, such as electro-osmotic flow, thermo-osmotic flow, diffusio-osmotic flow, or streaming current, is driven to a measured null condition. The null condition converts an otherwise hidden cross-coefficient into a ratio of imposed forces, while reciprocal symmetry checks separate true interfacial coupling from bulk artifacts. This active design follows inverse-modeling principles in which experiments are chosen to make hidden states observable [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2921417107), and uses flow and charge observables demonstrated at liquid-solid and electrocatalyst interfaces [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3028689404) [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4321480595).

**3. Interface-specific nonlocal Onsager identifiability model**

Represent the interface as a thin boundary with fluxes J_i(q, omega) = sum_j L_ij(q, omega) X_j(q, omega), where q and omega capture spatial nonlocality and memory. Derive identifiability conditions for broken-symmetry interfaces, such as charged, wetting-patterned, or catalytically active surfaces, showing which reciprocal null combinations uniquely recover electro-osmotic, thermo-osmotic, diffusio-osmotic, and related charge-transfer kernels. The model enforces reciprocity and non-negative entropy production but uses the null assay to avoid treating these constraints as generic regularization. Structural descriptors from reflectivity, infrared spectroscopy, and ellipsometry [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308711154) enter as known boundary parameters, while local conductivity and chemical-frictional maps [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4321480595) provide spatially varying coefficients.

**4. Physics-constrained inverse data assimilation with accelerated uncertainty updating**

Fuse the multimodal observations with the nonlocal interfacial model using inverse modeling that integrates physics-based equations with sensor data [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2921417107). Use accelerated Bayesian or ensemble uncertainty updating, adapted from transport in heterogeneous materials, to obtain posterior distributions of local kernels rather than point estimates [Author et al., 2012](https://openalex.org/W1982185356). The assimilation step also performs observability analysis, rejects perturbation sets that cannot separate coefficients, and propagates measurement noise into local flux uncertainty.

**5. Conservative flux-footprint upscaling and operando validation**

Convert the inferred kernel maps and local driving forces into local mass, heat, charge, and momentum flux densities. Integrate these heterogeneous fluxes over the interface using a conservative footprint or spatial-weighting scheme adapted from natural integration of scalar fluxes over complex terrain [Author et al., 1999](https://openalex.org/W2167962621). Validate the upscaled rates against independent macroscopic measurements in evaporation, evaporative cooling, or triple-phase catalysis, using interfacial wettability and mass-transfer characterization to define the operating microenvironment [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4223928347).
