# Degradation-Informed Design of Self-Compensating Chloride-Feedback Interphases for Stable Solid-State Sodium Energy Storage

## Motivation

The future of energy storage requires devices that are not only efficient and high-performing but also safe, resource-abundant, recyclable, and durable under intermittent renewable generation. Alternative storage carriers and device concepts are being explored for this reason, including formate-based electrochemical storage [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W4234705137) and computationally optimized redox-flow-battery electrodes [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W4242739072). At the same time, circularity and material recovery are becoming central design constraints, as shown by dielectrophoretic sorting of spent lithium-ion electrode materials [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4384282629). Among post-lithium options, solid-state sodium batteries are especially promising because sodium is more abundant than lithium and solid electrolytes can improve safety, and recent work on sulfide-chloride solid-state electrolytes with tunable anion ratios shows a path toward highly stable sodium cells [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4410507001). However, the key unresolved bottleneck is interfacial instability: local sodium flux, electronic leakage, chemomechanical stress, and uncontrolled interphase growth cause resistance rise and dendrite-like failure. Prior work improves materials or components, but it does not yet provide a coupled mechanistic design method that links ion/electron transport, degradation, and lifecycle constraints, a gap highlighted by multiscale process-systems thinking [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W4200367055). This idea directly addresses the previous novelty feedback by moving beyond a tunable sulfide-chloride composition: it introduces a general flux-balance stability criterion, a quantitative chloride-feedback constitutive law with a dimensionless feedback-gain number, and an anti-feedback negative control. The core cross-domain mechanism is degradation-informed Bayesian optimization, borrowed from computer science and product durability design [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4385617137), and adapted here to discover and validate interphases whose dynamic chloride redistribution stabilizes sodium-solid-electrolyte interfaces. The scientific advance is therefore not simply a new material stack, but a falsifiable mechanism and design loop for durable solid-state sodium storage.

## Method

This method is intended to solve the interfacial degradation bottleneck in solid-state sodium batteries for renewable energy storage; it can be divided into 4 parts: (1) a flux-balance and chloride-feedback model that defines a failure number F and a feedback-gain number Gamma; (2) a degradation-informed Bayesian co-design loop that uses accelerated aging data to infer F_c and Gamma_c without multi-parameter trial-and-error; (3) controlled fabrication of sulfide-chloride interphases with matched composition, thickness, and initial impedance but different feedback states; and (4) phased validation that first tests the single primary hypothesis that dynamic chloride redistribution causally suppresses dendrite onset and resistance growth, then tests scaling, anti-feedback necessity, and secondary lifecycle metrics.

**1. Flux-balance and chloride-feedback dimensionless model**

Build a phase-field/continuum model that couples local sodium deposition flux, chloride migration, interfacial passivation, electronic leakage, and chemomechanical stress. The model defines a local flux-balance number F = J_Na_loc / J_Cl_heal, where J_Na_loc is the local sodium flux at a protrusion or defect and J_Cl_heal is the chloride-mediated healing flux. The healing flux is written as J_Cl_heal = -D_Cl grad(c_Cl) - (D_Cl c_Cl / RT)(z_Cl F grad(phi) + Omega_Cl grad(sigma_h)) + kappa_fb c_Cl div(J_Na), combining ordinary diffusion, field-driven migration, stress-driven migration, and a feedback term that drives chloride toward regions of high sodium flux. A dimensionless feedback-gain number, Gamma = kappa_fb J_0 L / (D_Cl c_Cl0), distinguishes true self-compensation from a static graded-composition effect. The model predicts stable operation when F < F_c and Gamma > Gamma_c. This multiscale structure connects atomistic defect mobility, mesoscale interphase evolution, and cell-level transport, following the logic of multiscale electrochemical modeling [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W3164959115), while keeping safety, manufacturability, and resource constraints as explicit design objectives in the spirit of multiscale process systems engineering [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W4200367055).

**2. Degradation-informed Bayesian co-design loop**

Use degradation-informed Bayesian optimization as the experimental steering engine. Instead of optimizing only initial capacity or impedance, the loop treats time-to-dendrite-onset, impedance growth, and post-mortem chloride redistribution as primary objectives. A Gaussian-process surrogate is trained on accelerated aging experiments and physics-informed priors from the flux-balance model. The acquisition function selects the next single-variable experiment that maximally reduces uncertainty in F_c and Gamma_c. This is a direct adaptation of degradation-informed Bayesian optimization from durable product design [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4385617137). It also differs from earlier storage optimization efforts that mainly improve immediate device performance, such as neural-network and genetic-algorithm refinement of flow-battery electrodes [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W4242739072), because here degradation trajectories and failure thresholds are the central design signals.

**3. Controlled sulfide-chloride interphase fabrication**

Fabricate model interphases based on sulfide-chloride sodium solid electrolytes with tunable anion ratio [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4410507001). The average Cl/S ratio, interphase thickness, density, roughness, and initial area-specific resistance are held fixed across the primary comparison. The active factor is chloride feedback gain Gamma. Three primary states are made: FB-plus, a mobile-chloride graded interphase predicted to have positive feedback; FB-zero, the same initial graded interphase but with an ultrathin Na+-conducting, Cl-blocking barrier that suppresses chloride redistribution; and FB-sham, a barrier-containing control engineered to match ionic resistance while still permitting chloride transport. Additional controls include a homogeneous interphase with the same average Cl/S, a randomized multilayer with the same layer count and composition, a physical mixture of Cl-rich and S-rich powders, and single-layer Cl-rich or S-rich controls. Chloride motion is traced with 37Cl labeling and ToF-SIMS so that dynamic redistribution can be directly measured rather than inferred only from electrochemical performance.

**4. Mechanistic validation and scaling tests**

Validate the primary hypothesis under fixed current density, stack pressure, and temperature before expanding scope. The central prediction is that FB-plus cells remain stable longer than FB-zero cells even when initial impedance, average composition, microstructure, and contact mechanics are matched. After failure or scheduled interruption, cells are characterized by impedance spectroscopy, optical and electron microscopy, XPS, Raman, XRD, nanoindentation, and ToF-SIMS. Once the core mechanism is established, secondary variables are varied one at a time: current density, stack pressure, temperature, average Cl/S ratio, and interphase thickness. The goal is to test whether dendrite onset and rapid resistance growth collapse onto universal thresholds F_c and Gamma_c. Finally, an anti-feedback interphase is designed to have the same average composition and initial impedance but to drive chloride away from high-flux regions; if anti-feedback cells fail faster than neutral controls, the result rules out a purely static graded-composition explanation. This staged validation follows degradation-informed experimental design principles [Author et al., 2023](https://openalex.org/W4385617137) and keeps the mechanistic claim falsifiable.
