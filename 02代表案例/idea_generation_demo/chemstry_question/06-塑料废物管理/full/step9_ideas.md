# Presentation-Controlled Differentiable Mass-Balance Inference for Plastic Waste Sorting and Material-Flow Accounting

## Motivation

Better plastic-waste management requires reliable stream-level data and sorting decisions that remain valid for dirty, aged, contaminated, pigmented, multilayer, and #3-#7 plastics. Spectroscopic machine learning can characterize mixed plastic waste [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4309475252), NIR hyperspectral cascade detection can identify contaminants in post-consumer packaging [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W3176826653), NIR machine learning can recognize aged polypropylene [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4408188181), and AI-enabled sensor-fusion sorting targets economically viable handling of complex streams [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W7128697218). However, these approaches still mostly infer local labels or categories, while industrial streams contain overlapping, occluded, and presentation-dependent observations that must be converted into mass-balanced flow data for circular-economy decisions. Integrated material flow analysis provides the accounting structure needed to support material efficiency [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4286698685), remote sensing provides spectral unmixing and uncertainty-aware inversion for mixed scenes [Author et al., 2012](https://openalex.org/W2265885259), and hierarchical deep sorting shows how pairwise discrimination can be organized into circular plastic upgrading [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2988016874). The unresolved bottleneck is not another classifier but a controlled inference system that treats physical presentation as an active variable, conserves mass across unit operations, and quantifies identifiability limits.

## Method

This method is intended to solve robust stream-level characterization and material-flow accounting for mixed, occluded, contaminated, aged plastics; it can be divided into 4 parts: (1) a presentation-conditioned spectral unmixing observation model, (2) a differentiable mass-balance filter with conservation constraints and unit-operation transition kernels, (3) an identifiability-driven active presentation controller, and (4) a material-flow translation layer for recycling-yield and circular-economy decisions. The primary testable hypothesis is that uncertainty-directed active presentation reduces bulk-composition estimation error and sorting misallocation relative to fixed or random presentation under equal sensing, actuation, and throughput budgets.

**1. Presentation-conditioned spectral unmixing**

The observation model treats each conveyor view as a mixed scene rather than a clean isolated item, adapting remote-sensing unmixing and uncertainty-aware retrieval to plastic waste [Author et al., 2012](https://openalex.org/W2265885259). It ingests NIR, MIR, RGB, or false-color band ratios and explicitly conditions the likelihood on presentation variables such as singulation, overlap, layer thickness, belt speed, orientation, and view geometry. This extends prior spectroscopic plastic characterization [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4309475252), contaminant detection [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W3176826653), aged-polymer recognition [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4408188181), and sensor-fusion sorting [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W7128697218) by outputting posterior probabilities and calibrated uncertainties for polymer grade, contamination, aging, and recyclability states instead of only hard labels.

**2. Differentiable mass-balance filter**

A differentiable filter maintains nonnegative mass states for polymer grade, contamination level, aging state, and process stream, using integrated material-flow accounting as the physical backbone [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4286698685). The state transition equation couples unit operations such as singulation, washing, shredding, blending, rejection, and fuel diversion through learned but constrained transition kernels. Because the observation model is presentation-dependent, the filter jointly learns observation biases and transition kernels while enforcing mass conservation, nonnegativity, and known item-mass distributions. This separation allows the system to distinguish measurement error from true contaminant carryover or aging degradation.

**3. Identifiability-driven active presentation controller**

The controller makes presentation an actively controlled inference variable rather than a passive stratification label. It derives identifiability conditions and error bounds for recovering bulk mass composition from occluded bulk presentations using item-mass distributions, false-color channels, and presentation actions. At run time it selects the action that maximizes expected reduction in posterior mass-balance uncertainty per unit cost, such as slowing the belt, increasing singulation, reducing layer thickness, flipping or rescanning uncertain items, or diverting a small sample for higher-cost MIR analysis. The hierarchical escalation follows the deep-sorting logic of pairwise discrimination and selective re-inspection [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2988016874), while the uncertainty-based inversion follows remote-sensing retrieval practice [Author et al., 2012](https://openalex.org/W2265885259). Economic and throughput constraints are included as explicit costs, reflecting the need for viable industrial sorting [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W7128697218).

**4. Material-flow translation and decision support**

The posterior mass states are aggregated into material-flow accounts for input, recovered recyclate, reject, downcycled material, and waste-to-fuel streams. This layer converts item-level state probabilities into mass-balanced estimates of recycling yield, contamination risk, and circular-economy performance, following integrated MFA principles [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4286698685). It can support operational routing and policy-relevant scenarios such as bottle recovery, bag reduction, and plastics-to-fuel diversion, while preserving uncertainty rather than collapsing the stream into brittle point estimates.
