# Verification First-Passage Assay for the Verifiable Completion Boundary of the Periodic Table

## Motivation

The question whether the periodic table can ever be complete is not only a synthesis problem; it is also a verification problem because IUPAC/IUPAP recognition depends on evidence that can become extremely difficult to obtain for few short-lived atoms [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3048546628). Hot-fusion reaction surveys identify plausible projectile-target routes toward elements beyond Z = 118 and predict potentially accessible evaporation-residue cross sections, but entrance-channel effects, quasifission, and survival probabilities remain major obstacles [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3111464399). Theoretical predictions for superheavy stability must extrapolate nuclear interactions into sparsely constrained regions, so lifetimes and fission barriers are uncertain [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4415525127). At the same time, chemical identification beyond the heaviest studied elements is a single-atom thermalization and transport challenge [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4412961862). The field therefore lacks a quantitative estimand that separates a physical nuclear-existence limit from a verification limit. This proposal imports single-entity residence-time analysis from single-molecule separation science [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3087606226) and uncertainty-quantified reaction-network inference from Bayesian scientific machine learning [Author et al., 2024](https://openalex.org/W4392592391) to create a testable verification first-passage assay for superheavy-element discovery.

## Method

This method is intended to solve the absence of an experimentally anchored completion-boundary test for superheavy elements; it can be divided into 4 parts: (1) a verification first-passage time and minimum verification capability estimand, (2) an active modulated gas-flow transport discriminator, (3) a Bayesian single-event digital twin linking synthesis, survival, decay, transport, and detection, and (4) a decision assay that classifies candidate elements as physically limited or verification limited. The primary hypothesis is that structured modulation of transport/adsorption conditions causally increases the mutual information between single-atom observations and atomic-number-relevant chemical identity, relative to passive transport and decay-only evidence, while mean residence time and detection efficiency are held fixed.

**1. Verification first-passage estimand**

We define a latent state x = (Z, A, reaction channel) and event-level observables including decay-chain graph, decay energies/times, optional characteristic X-rays, and time-stamped transport/arrival data under a controlled intervention u. The verification first-passage time tau_v is the earliest time at which the posterior Bayes factor for one Z against the best competing Z exceeds a pre-registered threshold; the minimum verification capability C_min is the smallest expected production-detection budget required to reach that threshold with a specified probability. Non-stationary nuisance variables such as temperature drift, surface aging, charge-state changes, and detector dead time are included in the likelihood, making the estimand identifiable from coupled decay and residence-time data. This turns IUPAC-style discovery evidence into a quantitative, comparable boundary measure [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3048546628), and it uses first-passage statistics appropriate to rare single atoms in buffer-gas/transport systems [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4412961862) [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3087606226).

**2. Active modulated transport discriminator**

The transport stage is made into an active mechanistic discriminator by adding repeatable, structured modulation to a buffer-gas/atmospheric-pressure transfer line, for example segmented temperature zones, switchable surface chemistries, or periodic electric-field/gas-composition pulses, building on simulated superheavy gas-flow devices [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4412961862). Atoms with different adsorption enthalpies, diffusion coefficients, or charge-state equilibria then produce distinct residence-time and breakthrough distributions. The controlled quantity is the intervention-induced change in mutual information between residence-time/first-passage observables and atomic-number-relevant chemical identity. The modulation pattern is the controlled factor; mean flow, pressure, mean temperature, geometry, and detection window are held fixed so that any increase in information can be attributed to structured element-specific first-passage behavior, as in single-entity separation analysis [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3087606226).

**3. Bayesian single-event digital twin**

The inference engine is a Bayesian single-event digital twin adapted from uncertainty-quantified reaction-network discovery [Author et al., 2024](https://openalex.org/W4392592391). It represents the full pipeline as a stochastic network: entrance-channel capture, compound-nucleus formation, quasifission loss, evaporation-residue survival, decay, thermalization, transport, and detection. Production priors are constrained by hot-fusion reaction surveys and entrance-channel effects [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3111464399), while nuclear-stability and decay priors include model uncertainty from superheavy-interaction extrapolation [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4415525127). The model outputs posterior probabilities for Z/A, tau_v, C_min, and the probability of meeting the verification threshold under a given experimental configuration.

**4. Completion-boundary decision assay**

The completion-boundary assay uses the calibrated digital twin to evaluate candidate reactions and isotopes for Z > 118. If predicted nuclear existence probability, lifetime, or cross section falls below the verification requirement even under optimal transport, the candidate is classified as physically limited; if the nucleus could exist long enough but C_min exceeds plausible beam time, efficiency, or chemistry performance, it is verification limited. It also ranks calibration and beam-time actions by expected reduction in C_min or expected information gain. This directly addresses the periodic-table completion question by distinguishing nuclear-stability boundaries from experimental-verification boundaries under IUPAC-relevant evidence standards [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3048546628) and current synthesis predictions [Author et al., 2020](https://openalex.org/W3111464399) [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4415525127).
