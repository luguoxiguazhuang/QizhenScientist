# Causal Mechanism-State Model Predictive Planning for Repairable Retrosynthesis

## Motivation

AI can redefine chemistry only if planning systems move from isolated step prediction to robust, repairable multi-step synthesis. Template-free graph-to-sequence models showed that retrosynthesis and reaction prediction can be learned end-to-end [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W3206796875). Mechanism-aware bond-change models improve data efficiency and chemical plausibility for reaction outcomes [Author et al., 2024](https://openalex.org/W4392515737), and meta-learning addresses scarce selectivity data in asymmetric catalysis [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4409453348). MechRetro further demonstrates that chemical mechanism can make retrosynthesis and pathway planning more interpretable [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308632600). However, current planners still lack a causal intervention semantics for route repair: they do not predict how changing a protecting group, catalyst, purification, or workup will alter correlated mechanistic incompatibilities, nor do they plan under calibrated uncertainty. Predictive-control research provides the needed mechanism: learning-based model predictive control enables safe exploration with learned dynamics and explicit constraints [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2791704483), while the state-space versus direct multi-step predictor comparison motivates a controlled test of recursive mechanistic state models for sequential chemistry decisions [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4224330578).

## Method

This method is intended to solve uncertainty-brittle multi-step synthesis planning by making route repair an explicit causal control problem; it can be divided into 4 parts: a causal mechanism-state representation, a learnable incompatibility-graph learner, an intervention-conditioned transition model, and a receding-horizon constrained repair planner. The primary hypothesis is that, with frozen step predictors and matched model capacity, a recursive mechanism-state model with intervention variables predicts counterfactual route repairs and achieves higher validated route success than a direct multi-step route predictor or independent step aggregation. The mechanism is tested first by varying only the state-transition structure while holding data, predictors, constraints, and compute fixed.

**1. Causal mechanism-state representation with formal belief revision**

Represent each route as a trajectory of mechanistic state vectors s_t over molecular graphs, protecting-group occupancy, chemoselectivity masks, catalyst-condition compatibility, poison or impurity accumulation, and calibrated outcome beliefs. Repair actions are explicit causal interventions do(a), including reaction step, protecting-group change, catalyst substitution, purification, workup, or condition adjustment. A projection operator updates beliefs by enforcing chemical invariants: conflicting protecting groups cannot occupy the same site, catalyst-poison load is monotone unless removed by an explicit purification intervention, chemoselectivity masks remain disjoint, and bond changes must satisfy mechanism-enabled preconditions. This converts generic uncertainty into a chemistry-specific belief revision process rather than a hand-written failure flag, and it builds on mechanism-driven retrosynthetic graph learning [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308632600).

**2. Learnable mechanistic incompatibility graph**

Infer directed latent dependencies among state variables, reaction steps, and repair interventions from route outcomes, weak literature labels, expert annotations, and mechanism priors. The graph learner is constrained by temporal order, sparsity, and intervention identifiability so that edges represent testable causal dependencies such as catalyst poisoning, protecting-group lability, or chemoselectivity conflict, rather than only correlation. In data-sparse reaction families, the learner can reuse shared reaction features through meta-learning style adaptation [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4409453348). In the primary controlled phase the graph is planted; in later phases it is learned and compared against expert or planted graphs.

**3. Intervention-conditioned transition and calibration model**

Use frozen step-level predictors as observation models: template-free graph-to-sequence retrosynthesis and reaction prediction [Author et al., 2021](https://openalex.org/W3206796875), chemistry-aware bond-change outcome prediction [Author et al., 2024](https://openalex.org/W4392515737), selectivity prediction for low-data asymmetric catalysis [Author et al., 2025](https://openalex.org/W4409453348), and mechanism-driven retrosynthetic disconnection scoring [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4308632600). Train a compact transition function f_theta(s_t, do(a)) to predict the next mechanistic state, product distribution, yield-selectivity distribution, and constraint-violation probabilities. The recursive formulation is deliberately contrasted with direct multi-step endpoint prediction, following the predictive-control distinction between state-space models and direct multi-step predictors [Author et al., 2022](https://openalex.org/W4224330578).

**4. Safe receding-horizon repair planner**

Borrow learning-based model predictive control from control engineering to plan repair interventions over a finite horizon. At each decision point, the planner samples candidate action sequences, propagates them through the transition model, and selects the first action that maximizes expected route success while satisfying chance constraints on invariant violations and uncertainty thresholds. Safe exploration prioritizes repairs that are informative but unlikely to violate mechanistic feasibility, selectivity, or compatibility constraints. This adaptation gives synthesis planning a constrained sequential-decision structure rather than a one-shot route ranking [Author et al., 2019](https://openalex.org/W2791704483).
